Do countries that educate more girls have smaller families?

Using World Bank indicators from 1970 to 2019, we compare how many girls are enrolled in school with how many children women have, country by country and year by year.

Between 1990 and 2015, countries moved right and down: more girls in school, fewer births per woman

Each dot is one country that reports both measures in that year. Area is proportional to population; color shows whether girls (blue) or boys (red) have higher secondary enrollment. Gross enrollment can exceed 100% when students outside the official age range are enrolled. Aggregates such as “World” or income groups were removed.

Explore it yourself: watch every country move through time

Press Play or drag the slider to change the year. Hover a dot for details, click a dot (or search a country) to pin it and trace its whole path over time. Axes stay fixed so motion is comparable between years.

Data and transformations

  • Source: World Bank indicators (gender.csv and health.csv) from the light-and-salt repository.
  • Removed 48 World Bank aggregate codes (regions, income groups, “World”), leaving 217 countries.
  • Kept only country-years with both total fertility rate and the selected female enrollment measure; nothing was imputed.
  • Joined population (from health.csv) for dot size; computed enrollment gap = female minus male secondary enrollment.
  • The notebook oza3.ipynb in this repository shows the full preparation steps.

Design rationale

[TEAM: rewrite in your own words. Points to cover, drawn from the choices made in this build:]

  • Scatterplot with position on both axes for the two variables being related, because position is the most accurate channel for judging a relationship.
  • Area for population so large countries (China, India) are visible without hiding small ones; diverging red–blue color for the girls-minus-boys enrollment gap, centred on parity.
  • Animation over year (play button + slider) because the story is movement; fixed axes so motion is comparable.
  • Details on demand (tooltip) rather than permanent labels to limit clutter; pinning with trails to follow single countries.
  • Linked correlation chart that doubles as a year selector.
